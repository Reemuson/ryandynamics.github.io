Choose materials based on expected load and temperature, not just printability. sections: - title: What is creep and why it matters body: >- Material creep is the tendency of a material to deform gradually under sustained stress, often accelerated by temperature. It is typically discussed in terms of strain (elongation) over time. For 3D-printed functional parts, such as hinges, clips, fixtures and brackets, creep shows up as parts that relax, sag, or drift out of tolerance under a constant load or in a warm environment. - title: The study in plain English body: >- A paper by Oguz Dogan et al. examined creep behaviour of common FFF materials (PLA, ABS, ASA/TPLA/CPE/Nylon/PC) under different loads and temperatures, using ASTM D2990-17 procedures for specimen prep and measurement. The headline trends: items: - text: Higher load and/or higher temperature increased elongation (strain) for all tested plastics. - text: Load was generally a stronger driver of elongation than temperature. - text: PLA had the poorest creep resistance; best reserved for room-temp, lightly loaded applications. - text: ABS/ASA were reasonable choices for moderate loads/temperatures, but not for severe conditions. - text: Nylon elongated substantially with load/heat yet did not rupture under the test conditions. - text: PC showed the highest resistance to creep among those tested; suitable for higher loads/temperatures. table: compact: true headers: [Material (FFF), Relative creep resistance (study summary), Typical use guidance] rows: - cells: [PLA, Low, "Models, jigs away from heat, light duty"] - cells: [ABS / ASA, Medium, "Moderate load, moderate temp; ASA for outdoor UV"] - cells: [Nylon, "Medium-low (high elongation, no rupture)", Tough parts where some drift is acceptable] - cells: [PC, High, Functional parts in warm/service-load environments] - title: Design implications & recommendations items: - { label: Match material to service conditions, text: "choose PC (or PC-blend) for warm, loaded parts; ABS/ASA for general fixtures; keep PLA for display or low-load room-temp parts." } - { label: Reduce sustained stress, text: "shorten unsupported spans, use ribs/fillets, and spread load over larger contact area." } - { label: Mind temperature, text: "keep functional parts away from heat sources; enclosures can trap warmth and accelerate creep." } - { label: Orient for strength, text: "align layer lines with the principal load where possible to reduce through-layer deformation." } - { label: Test a coupon, text: "for critical uses, print a small test bar and hang a representative load for 24-72 h; measure drift before committing." } - title: Applying this in real projects body: >- Understanding creep helps you choose the right material and geometry before you print.
